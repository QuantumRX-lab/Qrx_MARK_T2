# Need Statement

## Why this exists

A father and his 7-year-old daughter want to build a game together. She plays
Roblox on an iPad. The game is a combative multiplayer Pac-Man: drive a
Pac-Man-shaped vehicle through a multi-level maze, eat pellets, bank them, buy
upgrades, and fight other players by direction — mouth is the weapon, flank and
back are the weak spots.

The need is not "a game exists at the end". It is:

1. **A game good enough to be played voluntarily**, by the child, when nobody
   is asking her to test anything.
2. **A build she has a real hand in**, rather than one she watched being typed.
3. **A thing that stays fair** when a seven-year-old and an adult are in the
   same lobby.

Those three pull against each other, and most of the decisions in this tree are
about where they trade.

## What success looks like

- She opens it on the iPad on a Tuesday afternoon without being prompted.
- She can name something in it that is hers — a map, a ghost, a colour, a
  number she chose.
- An adult can play it properly and not be bored.
- Nothing in it can take her progress away in a way that makes her cry.

## What this is not

- Not a commercial product. No monetisation, no Robux, no marketplace.
- Not public. Private during development, Friends-only afterwards.
- Not a photoreal or large-scale simulation — the platform's ceiling and the
  iPad's headroom set the bounds, and both are fine for this.

## Constraints taken as given

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | Roblox. Free to publish, free hosting, free cross-platform, free netcode |
| Reference device | **iPad.** It is where the game is actually played, so it is the performance and control reference, not a port target |
| Also runs on | The family laptop, for building in Studio |
| Language | Luau. Sandboxed: no filesystem, no sockets, no native code |
| Budget | Zero |
| Team | One adult building the engine, one child building content, one external agent designing vehicles |
| Audience | The family and their friends |
